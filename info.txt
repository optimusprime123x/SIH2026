SIH 2026 — Team Brief (PS 26034)

PROBLEM STATEMENT (official, in full)

Problem Statement ID: 26034

Problem Statement Title: Software System to check compliance of Packaged Commodities under Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 by scanning products, images and labels.

Description:

Background:

Packaged commodities are widely sold through retail stores, supermarkets and e-commerce platforms across India. Under the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, every packaged commodity is required to bear mandatory declarations such as name and address of manufacturer/packer/importer, net quantity, Maximum Retail Price (MRP), month and year of manufacture/packing/import, consumer care details and other prescribed declarations in a specified format and manner. These declarations are important for ensuring transparency, fair trade practices and consumer protection. However, due to the large volume and variety of packaged products available in the market, manual inspection and compliance checking by enforcement agencies becomes time-consuming and resource intensive. Non-compliance such as missing declarations, incorrect font sizes, improper MRP declarations and other such practices are frequently observed. There is scope to develop a compliance checking system capable of scanning product labels, package images and product listings to identify violations under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. Accordingly, a software system capable of automatically detecting, extracting and validating mandatory declarations and identifying noncompliances in packaged commodities through image and label analysis can be developed.

Description:

Develop a software application capable of scanning packaged commodity labels, product images and product information to automatically assess compliance with the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011.

The system should be capable of:

• Scanning and analyzing images of packaged commodities.

• Detecting mandatory declarations prescribed under Legal Metrology rules.

• Checking correctness, completeness and placement of declarations.

• Identifying missing or non-compliant declarations.

• Checking readability and font size requirements.

• Generating compliance reports and violation summaries.

• Maintaining a repository of scanned products and compliance history.

• Providing dashboards for enforcement officials.

Expected Solution:

The proposed solution should include:

• User-friendly web and/or mobile-based software application.

• Automated extraction and validation of mandatory declarations.

• Rule-based compliance checking for Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011.

• Generation of digital compliance reports in PDF and editable formats.

• Dashboard for monitoring inspections, violations and product compliance details.

• Search and retrieval facility for previously scanned products and reports.

• Technical documentation describing software architecture and deployment framework.

Key Functional Requirements:

• Image upload and product scanning functionality.

• Extraction of declarations from labels and packaging and detection of mandatory declarations

• Font size and readability analysis.

• Detection of missing, misleading or non-standard declarations.

• Generation of compliance/non-compliance reports.

• Attachment of photographs and supporting evidence.

• Repository of scanned products and inspection history.

• Role-based user access and secure authentication.

• Dashboard for monitoring compliance status and enforcement activities.

• Export of reports to PDF and editable formats.

OUR APPROACH

What we're building: An inspector takes a photo of a product (or submits an e-commerce listing). The app reads the label, checks it against the actual 2011 Rules, and produces a report listing exactly what's compliant and what's violated, with the legal clause number for each violation. Reports export to PDF and Word. Every scan is saved, so there's a searchable history of all inspections, plus a dashboard showing compliance stats. Login with roles (inspector / admin).

How it works — three layers:



Extract. The photo goes to an AI vision model (Gemini). Its only job is reading: it returns the label details in structured form — MRP value, net quantity, dates, manufacturer details, what's present, what's missing, and where each item sits on the label. It does not decide compliance.

Validate. Our own rules engine checks those details against the law. We encode every relevant rule from the 2011 Rules as a code check — MRP must say "inclusive of all taxes", quantity must use standard units (g, not gms), date in the correct format, all six mandatory declarations present, text height above the legal minimum. Every violation carries its exact clause number. This layer is deterministic: same label, same verdict, every time. This is what makes it a genuine rule-based enforcement tool, not an AI wrapper.

Review. The inspector confirms or overrides the result before the final report is generated. Overrides are logged with reasons. The system supports the inspector's decision; it doesn't replace it.

Font size — our special feature: The law prescribes minimum letter heights in millimeters, based on package size. A photo has no real-world scale, so no AI can measure mm directly. Our solution: the inspector places a ₹10 coin (exactly 27mm) or a standard card in the frame, and we compute real sizes from pixels using it. Without calibration, the system still gives an indicative relative check. Most teams will skip this requirement entirely; we solve it properly.

E-commerce mode: The PS explicitly mentions product listings. We add a mode where a Flipkart/Amazon listing URL is checked for its declared MRP, net quantity, and manufacturer details.

Tech stack (fully free tier): Cloudflare Workers (backend + API), D1 (database — products, inspections, users, violation history), R2 (photo and evidence storage), Gemini API via Cloudflare AI Gateway (label extraction), report generation in the browser (PDF + docx). Running cost: zero.

Why this wins: Many teams will pick this PS because it looks easy, and most will just forward the image to an AI and display its answer. We stand apart on three visible things: a real rules engine with clause citations in every report, actual millimeter-accurate font measurement via calibration, and listing checks beyond just photos.



1. Core declarations on a retail package

Information to detect	Legal provision	What must be declared

Manufacturer / packer / importer	Rule 6(1)(a); Rule 10(1)–(2)	Name and complete address of the manufacturer. If different, both manufacturer and packer. For imports, importer name and address.

Country of origin	Rule 6(1)(aa)	For imported products: country of origin, manufacture or assembly.

Product identity	Rule 6(1)(b)	Common or generic name of the commodity. For packages containing multiple products, name and number/quantity of each product.

Net quantity	Rule 6(1)(c); Rules 11–13	Net weight, measure or number, excluding packaging and wrappers.

Month and year of manufacture	Rule 6(1)(d)	Month and year of manufacture. Importantly, since the 2021 amendment, “pre-packed or imported” was removed: the current general rule requires the manufacture month and year.

Best-before / use-by date	Rule 6(1)(da)	Required where the commodity may become unfit for human consumption after a period of time.

MRP	Rule 6(1)(e)	Retail sale price clearly identified as the maximum retail price, inclusive of all taxes, in Indian currency.

Product dimensions	Rule 6(1)(f); Rules 14–17	Dimensions where size is relevant, including dimensions of different pieces where applicable.

Consumer-care details	Rule 6(2)	Name, address, telephone number and email address of the person or office handling consumer complaints.

Unit sale price	Rule 6(11)	Price per gram/kilogram, centimetre/metre, millilitre/litre, or per number/unit, as applicable.



The consolidated Rules list these declarations under Rule 6 and provide their detailed formatting requirements in Rules 7–17. Consolidated Packaged Commodities Rules



2. Exact requirements for each declaration

Manufacturer, packer and importer — Rule 6(1)(a), Rule 10



The label must identify:



manufacturer;

packer, if different from the manufacturer;

importer, for imported packages; and

complete address.



If the package is manufactured outside India but packed in India, the Indian packer or importer’s name and complete address must also appear on the principal display panel.



For packages of capacity up to 10 cubic centimetres, a mark or inscription that allows the consumer to identify the responsible party may be sufficient under Rule 10(1).



Country of origin — Rule 6(1)(aa)



Imported products must state the country of origin, manufacture or assembly.



This is a separate declaration from the importer’s name and address.



Common or generic product name — Rule 6(1)(b)



The product must be identified using its common or generic name. A brand name alone may not be sufficient.



For multi-product packages, the name and number or quantity of each product must be stated.



Net quantity — Rule 6(1)(c), Rules 11–13



The declared quantity must:



exclude wrappers and packaging;

use the correct standard unit;

correspond to the quantity received by the consumer, subject to permitted environmental variation;

not use misleading wording such as “about”, “approximately”, “minimum” or “not less than”; and

include supplementary quantity information for multipacks.



Rule 13 requires the use of the International System of Units. Examples include:



less than 1 kg: grams;

1 kg or more: kilograms;

less than 1 litre: millilitres;

1 litre or more: litres;

less than 1 metre: centimetres;

1 metre or more: metres.



The Rules do not specifically use the phrase “gms is prohibited”, but informal or non-standard units should be flagged because Rule 13(5)(i) requires SI units.



Manufacture date — Rule 6(1)(d)



The current general requirement is the month and year of manufacture.



The team brief should therefore avoid saying that every package must show the month/year of “manufacture, packing or import”. That was the earlier wording. The words “or pre-packed or imported” were removed by the 2021 amendment, effective from 1 October 2022. Government explanation of the amendment



Important exceptions or sectoral overrides include:



food: Food Safety and Standards legislation applies;

certified seeds: Seeds legislation applies;

cosmetics: Drugs and Cosmetics Rules apply;

certain servicing spare parts and accessories are exempt where used under warranty and not sold to end customers;

electronic products, spare parts and accessories must show the manufacture month and year visibly and legibly anywhere on the retail package.



The spare-parts amendment took effect from 1 April 2024. 2023 Amendment Rules



Best-before / use-by — Rule 6(1)(da)



This is required where the product may become unfit for human consumption after a period of time.



The Rule distinguishes:



“best before”: the end of the period during which the product remains marketable and retains its expected qualities;

“use by”: the end of the period after which the product should not be regarded as marketable.



The requirement does not apply where another law already governs the relevant date.



MRP — Rule 6(1)(e)



The declaration must make clear that the price is the maximum retail price inclusive of all taxes and must be in Indian currency.



Examples that should normally be accepted by the system include:



MRP ₹100 inclusive of all taxes

Maximum Retail Price ₹100 incl. of all taxes

MRP Rs. 100 inclusive of all taxes



The following should be flagged:



MRP without an indication that it includes all taxes;

an MRP in a foreign currency;

a price higher than the declared MRP being charged;

different MRPs on identical packages where used as a restrictive or unfair trade practice under Rule 18(2A).



There are limited exceptions for certain alcoholic beverages, bidis and domestic LPG cylinders covered by the Government’s Administrative Price Mechanism.



Dimensions — Rule 6(1)(f), Rules 14–17



Dimensions are required where the size of the commodity is relevant.



Specific examples include:



bed sheets, towels, sarees, fabrics and similar goods — Rule 14;

products whose dimensions or weight affect price — Rule 15;

aluminium foil, facial tissues, toilet paper and similar sheets — Rule 16;

bags, boxes, cups, pans and container-type products — Rule 17.



For textiles or similar products, the package may need to state:



number of pieces;

finished dimensions;

dimensions and MRP of each differently sized piece; and

dimensions and MRP on each individual piece.

Consumer-care information — Rule 6(2)



The package must provide:



name;

address;

telephone number; and

email address



of the person or office that can be contacted for consumer complaints.



Your system should not treat a generic brand website alone as automatically sufficient unless it clearly provides the required consumer-contact route.



Unit sale price — Rule 6(11)



The unit sale price must be expressed in rupees, rounded to two decimal places:



per gram if quantity is below 1 kg;

per kilogram if quantity is above 1 kg;

per centimetre if length is below 1 metre;

per metre if length is above 1 metre;

per millilitre if volume is below 1 litre;

per litre if volume is above 1 litre; or

per number/unit for products sold by number.



It is not required where:



the MRP equals the unit sale price;

the package is a combination, group or multi-piece package; or

the alcoholic-beverage exception applies.



The combination/group/multi-piece exception was added by the 2023 amendment. 2023 Amendment Rules

These should also be included in your rule database:



Declaration	Clause

4. Placement, font size and readability

Placement — Rule 8



Every declaration must appear on the principal display panel.



The quantity declaration must have clear space around it:



above and below: at least the height of the numeral;

left and right: at least twice the height of the numeral.

Font size — Rule 7(2)–(3)



The minimum height applies to numerals and letters in declarations.



Principal display panel area	Normal minimum height	Blown/formed/moulded lettering

Up to 50 cm²	1.0 mm	2.0 mm

More than 50 to 100 cm²	1.5 mm	3.0 mm

More than 100 to 500 cm²	2.5 mm	4.0 mm

More than 500 to 2,500 cm²	4.0 mm	6.0 mm

More than 2,500 cm²	6.0 mm	6.0 mm



Letter or numeral width must generally be at least one-third of its height, except for 1, i, I and l.



The principal display panel area is calculated differently for rectangular, cylindrical and irregular packages under Rule 7(4). Your calibration feature is therefore genuinely useful. However, the ₹10 coin or card is an engineering method chosen by your system; it is not itself prescribed by the Rules.



Readability and contrast — Rule 9



Declarations must be:



legible;

prominent;

clear and unambiguous;

in a contrasting colour for MRP and net quantity; and

readable without looking through the product liquid.



Declarations must generally be in Hindi using Devanagari script or English. Additional languages are allowed.



For medical devices, the 2025 amendment redirects declaration format and font requirements to the Medical Devices Rules, 2017. Department of Consumer Affairs announcement



5. E-commerce declarations



There are now two separate digital requirements.



Digital display — Rule 6(10)



An e-commerce entity must display the mandatory Rule 6(1) declarations on its digital network, except the month and year of manufacture or packing.



For marketplace platforms, responsibility for correctness generally remains with the manufacturer, seller, dealer or importer where the platform is merely acting as an intermediary and satisfies the applicable conditions.



Country-of-origin filter — Rule 6(10A)



From 1 July 2026, every e-commerce entity selling imported products must provide product listings in a searchable and sortable filter specifying the country of origin.



This is a platform-functionality requirement, not merely a requirement to display country of origin somewhere in the product description. 2026 amendment text



6. Wholesale-package declarations — Rule 24



A wholesale package must state:



name and address of the manufacturer/importer/packer;

identity of the commodity; and

total number of retail packages inside, or the net quantity in standard units.



This is separate from the retail-package declaration regime. Rule 24 text



7. Special exemptions and alternative declarations



Rule 26 contains important exceptions:



packages of 10 g or 10 ml or less, subject to specific exceptions;

fast food packed by restaurants or hotels;

certain drug formulations;

thread sold in coils to handloom weavers;

garments or hosiery sold loose/open, provided specified manufacturer, consumer-care, size and MRP information is supplied.



For loose garments or hosiery, the required details include:



manufacturer/marketer/brand owner/importer and country of origin where imported;

consumer-care email and phone;

size and metric dimensions;

MRP inclusive of all taxes in Indian currency; and

the same information on the e-commerce website where sold online.



From 1 February 2026, the small-package exemption in Rule 26(a) does not apply to pan masala. Official Government announcement



8. Advertisements



If an advertisement mentions the retail sale price, it must also state the net quantity or number of items under Rule 31(1).



The font size of the net quantity or number must be the same as the font size of the retail sale price under Rule 31(2). Rule 31 text



Important corrections to your current approach



Your brief currently says “all six mandatory declarations”. That is too narrow. A robust system should separately model at least:



responsible party;

country of origin;

product name;

net quantity;

manufacture date;

best-before/use-by date;

MRP;

dimensions;

consumer-care details;

unit sale price;

special declarations such as GM or vegetarian/non-vegetarian markings;

e-commerce display and country-of-origin filtering.



Also, change “month and year of manufacture/packing/import” to “month and year of manufacture” for the current general rule. Packing/import dates were part of the earlier wording but were removed by the 2021 amendment.

6(1)(a), 6(1)(aa), 6(1)(b), 6(1)(c), 6(1)(d), 6(1)(da), 6(1)(e), 6(1)(f), 6(2), 6(7), 6(8), 6(10), 6(10A), 6(11), 7(2), 7(3), 8(1), 9(1), 10(1), 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 24, 26, and 31.
